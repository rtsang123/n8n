The One Idea

Don't manage your time. Audit it — then buy it back.

Every task on your plate has a price. If a task is worth less than your buyback rate, you should never do it again. AI is the cheapest "hire" that has ever existed for this kind of work.

Example: A cleaning company owner spent 6 hours a week replying to booking emails. An AI assistant now drafts every reply; she reviews and hits send in 40 minutes. That's 5+ hours back — every single week.

Your 10-Minute Quick Win
  • Open your sent email and calendar from last week
  • List every task you touched more than 3 times (page 4 worksheet)
  • Mark each one: Delete, AI, Delegate, or Keep
  • Count the hours next to "Delete" and "AI" — that's your buyback target

The Action Plan

Five steps to your first 10 hours

1

Run the time audit

List everything you did last week that took 30+ minutes. Your sent folder and calendar don't lie.

Do this: Fill in the worksheet on page 4. Aim for 10–15 tasks.

2

Calculate your buyback rate

Take what an hour of your time is worth and divide by four. Any task below that number is stealing from you.

Do this: Write your rate at the top of the worksheet. Example: $200/hr ÷ 4 = $50.

3

Tag every task: Delete, AI, Delegate, or Keep

Delete what nobody would miss. Tag "AI" on anything repetitive with words or data. Delegate what needs a human. Keep only what truly needs you.

Do this: One tag per row. No blanks allowed.

4

Automate your #1 "AI" task today

Pick the AI-tagged task with the most hours. For most owners it's email. Use the prompt on page 4 to set up your reply assistant in 10 minutes.

Do this: Paste the prompt into ChatGPT or Claude and test it on 3 real emails.

5

Protect the hours you just freed

Saved time evaporates unless you claim it. Block the freed hours for the work only you can do — sales, hiring, strategy, or actually going home.

Do this: Add a recurring calendar block named "Bought-back time."

Common Mistakes

This is where most business owners get stuck.

They audit from memory instead of the calendar — and miss half the leaks. They keep tasks because "clients expect me personally." And they free up 5 hours, then fill them with more busywork.

Pro Tip

Don't aim for perfect AI drafts — aim for 80% drafts you edit in 30 seconds. Owners who wait for perfect automation save nothing. Owners who accept "almost right, instantly" save 10 hours a week.

The Email Buyback Prompt

You are my email assistant at [BUSINESS NAME], a [WHAT YOU DO] business. Our tone is friendly, brief, and professional. When I paste a customer email, draft a reply that: 1) answers their question directly, 2) offers the next step (usually [BOOKING LINK / QUOTE / CALL]), 3) stays under 120 words. If information is missing, ask me one clarifying question instead of guessing. Here's the email: [PASTE EMAIL]

The "Should I Keep This Task?" Test

  • Would a customer notice — or care — if AI did the first draft? If no: AI.
  • Does it follow the same pattern every time? If yes: AI.
  • Does it need my judgment, relationships, or signature? If yes: Keep (but let AI prep it).
  • Would anything break if it stopped entirely? If no: Delete.
