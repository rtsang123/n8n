The One Idea

The R-T-C-F Formula: Role, Task, Context, Format

Role: who the AI should be. Task: exactly what you want. Context: your business, customer, and tone. Format: what the answer should look like. Miss one and quality drops fast.

Example: Instead of "write a follow-up email," try: "You are my sales assistant (Role). Write a follow-up to a quote we sent Tuesday (Task). We're a roofing company; the client worried about price (Context). Under 100 words, friendly, one clear next step (Format)."

The Action Plan

Five steps to prompts that sound like you

1

Write your Business Brief once

Three sentences: what you do, who you serve, how you sound. You'll paste this into every prompt as instant context.

Do this: Fill in the Business Brief template on page 4. Save it in your notes app.

2

Run your first prompt on real work

Not a test. A real email, a real review, a real follow-up sitting in your queue right now. Real stakes show real value.

Do this: Use Prompt #1 or #2 from page 4 on something due today.

3

Improve the output — don't restart

First drafts are 80% there. Reply with "shorter," "warmer," or "add a deadline" instead of writing a new prompt. Each reply sharpens it.

Do this: Give at least two follow-up instructions before you judge the result.

4

Save your winners in a prompt library

When a prompt produces something you actually sent, it's an asset. Store it where you and your team can reuse it.

Do this: Create one note titled "Prompt Library." Paste tweaked winners with a one-line label.

5

Make it a daily default

New habit rule: before doing any writing task by hand, ask "did I try the prompt first?" Two weeks of this and it's automatic.

Do this: Pin your AI tool as the first tab in your browser.

Common Mistakes

This is where most business owners get stuck.

They write one-line prompts and expect mind reading. They quit after one mediocre draft instead of replying "make it better." And they never save what worked — so every day starts from zero.

Pro Tip

End tricky prompts with: "Ask me three questions before you answer." The AI fills its own context gaps, and the quality jump feels like upgrading to a better model — for free.

Copy / Paste Assets

Your Business Brief + the Starter Seven

The Business Brief (fill once, paste everywhere)

My business: [NAME], a [WHAT YOU DO] serving [WHO] in [AREA]. Our tone: [e.g. friendly, plain-spoken, professional]. Never use hype words or jargon.

1 · Email Reply

[Business Brief] Draft a reply to this customer email. Answer their question, offer [NEXT STEP], stay under 120 words: [PASTE EMAIL]

2 · Quote Follow-Up

[Business Brief] Write a friendly follow-up on a quote sent [WHEN] for [SERVICE]. No pressure tone, one clear question, under 90 words.

3 · Review Reply

[Business Brief] Reply to this [5-STAR / CRITICAL] review. Thank them, sound human not corporate, under 60 words: [PASTE REVIEW]

4 · Meeting Summary

[Business Brief] Turn these rough notes into: decisions made, action items with owners, and open questions. Bullet points only: [PASTE NOTES]

5 · SOP Writer

[Business Brief] I'll describe how I do a task. Turn it into a numbered SOP a brand-new hire could follow, with a checklist at the end: [DESCRIBE TASK]

6 · Content Ideas

[Business Brief] Give me 10 post ideas answering real questions [WHO] ask before buying. For each: a hook line and a 2-sentence outline.

7 · The Explainer

[Business Brief] Rewrite this so a busy customer instantly gets it. Grade 6 reading level, no jargon, half the length: [PASTE TEXT]

Every prompt here already has Role, Task, Context, and Format built in. When you write your own, check all four are present before blaming the AI.
