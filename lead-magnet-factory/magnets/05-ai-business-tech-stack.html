The One Idea

Build in layers, not in shopping sprees.

Tools compound when each one feeds the next: your notetaker feeds your assistant, your assistant feeds your automations. Bought all at once, they just compete for your attention and lose.

Example: A consultant started with just ChatGPT (layer 1) for a month, then added a meeting notetaker (layer 2). Now every client call auto-becomes notes, a follow-up email, and a task list — a system, not a pile of apps.

The Action Plan

Five layers, added in order

1

The Assistant — your AI home base

ChatGPT or Claude, paid tier. This is where drafts, ideas, and decisions happen. If you only ever do one thing, do this.

Do this: Subscribe (~$20/mo), pin it as your first browser tab, use it daily for two weeks.

2

The Notetaker — never scribble in meetings again

Tools like Fathom, Otter, or Fireflies join your calls and deliver notes, action items, and summaries automatically.

Do this: Connect it to your calendar. Let it join every call for one week, then read the summaries.

3

The Automation Hub — connect your apps

n8n, Zapier, or Make moves data between your tools while you sleep: form fills become CRM entries, invoices trigger reminders.

Do this: Build one automation only: "new lead → instant reply + notification to me."

4

The Content Engine — one idea becomes ten posts

Your assistant plus a scheduler (Buffer, Later, or similar). Record one voice memo, turn it into a week of posts and an email.

Do this: Use Prompt Pack guide #04 to repurpose one client conversation into three posts.

5

The Smart CRM — follow-ups that never slip

A simple CRM (HubSpot free tier works) with AI drafting your follow-ups. Leads stop dying in your inbox.

Do this: Import your last 20 leads. Set one rule: every lead gets a follow-up within 24 hours.

Common Mistakes

This is where most business owners get stuck.

They buy layer 3 tools before layer 1 habits exist. They chase every new tool launch instead of mastering a boring stack. And they judge tools in a weekend instead of a two-week real-work trial.

Pro Tip

Put a monthly "stack audit" in your calendar: every tool must name the hours it saved that month. Can't answer? It's cancelled. A five-tool stack you use beats a fifteen-tool stack you pay for.

Before You Buy — the 4 Checks

  • Does it replace hours, not just look cool?
  • Does it connect to what I already use?
