The One Idea

Pass the 5-Second Test, win the visitor.

In five seconds, a stranger must know three things: what you do, who it's for, and what to do next. If any of the three is missing, everything below it is wasted.

Example: A plumbing company's hero said "Quality You Can Trust." Nobody knew what they did. Changed to "Same-Day Plumbing Repairs in Durham — Book in 60 Seconds" with a review badge beside it. Calls went up the same week.

Your 10-Minute Quick Win
  • Open your homepage on your phone (that's where most visitors are)
  • Show it to someone for 5 seconds, then ask: what do we do, and what should they click?
  • Run the 10-point checklist on page 4 and total your score
  • Circle your lowest-scoring check — that's this week's fix

The Action Plan

Five steps from leaky to lead machine

1

Run the 5-second test

Fresh eyes only — a friend, your spouse, anyone who doesn't work in the business. Five seconds on your homepage, then two questions.

Do this: Ask: "What do we do?" and "What would you click?" Write down exactly what they say.

2

Score the 10-point audit

Page 4 has ten yes-or-no checks covering clarity, proof, and friction. Be honest — a generous score just hides the leak.

Do this: Tick each box, total your score out of 10.

3

Fix the hero first

Your headline should name the outcome, the audience, and the next step. Use the headline formula on page 4 — or let AI draft five options.

Do this: Write a headline in this shape: "[Result] for [who] in [area] — [one clear action]."

4

Put proof above the fold

Reviews, star ratings, years in business, jobs completed, real client names. Trust you've already earned is useless if it's hiding on page three.

Do this: Move your best review and your strongest number next to the headline.

5

Cut contact friction to one step

Every extra form field and click costs you leads. Capture the lead first; gather the details on the call.

Do this: Reduce your form to name, phone/email, and one question — or add a booking link.

Common Mistakes

This is where most business owners get stuck.

They rebuild the whole site when three fixes would do. They write headlines about themselves instead of the customer's problem. And they send hard-won visitors to a contact form with nine required fields.

Pro Tip

Check your site on your phone with your slowest connection. If it takes more than three seconds to load or the button is hard to tap with a thumb, fix that before touching a single word of copy.
