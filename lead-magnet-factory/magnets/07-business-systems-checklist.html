The One Idea

Talk it out. Let AI write it down.

The reason your processes aren't documented isn't laziness — it's that writing is slow. So don't write. Explain the task out loud like you're training a new hire, record it, and let AI turn the transcript into a clean step-by-step SOP.

Example: An HVAC owner spent years "meaning to" document his estimate process. He voice-recorded himself explaining it on a drive between jobs — nine minutes. AI turned it into a two-page SOP with a checklist. His new tech quoted his first job solo that same week.

The Action Plan

Five steps from "in my head" to "on paper"

1

Score the eight systems

For each system, three questions: Is it written down? Does someone besides you own it? Is any part automated? One point each.

Do this: Fill in the checklist on page 4. Total each row out of 3.

2

Pick ONE system — your weakest that hurts

Lowest score wins. Tie-breaker: which one would break first if you took two weeks off?

Do this: Circle one row. Ignore the other seven for now.

3

Record yourself doing or explaining it

Voice memo, phone screen recording, or a Loom while you do the task. Talk like you're training a smart new hire. Ten minutes max.

Do this: Record it today — perfection is the enemy, rambling is fine.

4

Let AI turn it into an SOP

Paste the transcript into the SOP Builder prompt on page 4. You'll get numbered steps, a checklist, and the common failure points — in seconds.

Do this: Run the prompt, read it once, fix anything AI got wrong.

5

Store it where the team actually looks

One home for all SOPs: Google Drive folder, Notion page, wherever your team already lives. A system nobody can find doesn't exist.

Do this: Create the folder, drop in SOP #1, and tell the team where it lives.

Common Mistakes

This is where most business owners get stuck.

They try to document all eight systems in one heroic weekend — and burn out by Sunday. They write SOPs nobody can find. And they document the process but never hand it to anyone, so they're still the bottleneck.

Pro Tip

Make it a rhythm: one system per week, recorded on your commute or between jobs. In eight weeks you'll have a business that runs on paper instead of memory — that's what buyers pay premiums for, and what lets you finally take that vacation.

The SOP Builder Prompt

You are an operations manager for a [WHAT YOU DO] business. Below is a transcript of me explaining how we do: [TASK NAME]. Turn it into an SOP a brand-new hire could follow with zero questions: 1) numbered steps in order, 2) tools or logins needed at each step, 3) a "done right looks like" checklist, 4) the 3 most likely mistakes and how to avoid them. Keep it under one page. Transcript: [PASTE TRANSCRIPT]

The Vacation Test

  • Could a new hire find how we do this in under 2 minutes?
  • Would this process survive me being unreachable for 2 weeks?
  • Does someone other than me own the outcome?
  • If they left tomorrow, is the knowledge still in the business?

Any "no" above = that system still depends on a person, not a process.
