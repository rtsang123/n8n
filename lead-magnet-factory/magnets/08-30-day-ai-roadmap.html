You don't need an AI strategy. You need an AI month.

The One Idea

Habit first. Wins second. Automation third. Proof last.

Most owners start with automation and stall — you can't automate a tool you don't use. This order works because each week stands on the one before it.

Example: A design agency owner followed this exact order. Week 1 felt too easy — just using AI daily. By week 3 she automated proposal first drafts. Day 30 tally: seven hours a week back, and a team that stopped rolling their eyes at AI.

Your 10-Minute Quick Win (this is Day 1)
  • Subscribe to ChatGPT or Claude (~$20/mo) and pin it as your first browser tab
  • Write your 3-sentence Business Brief (template on page 4)
  • Use it on one real task from today's to-do list
  • Put "AI hour" on your calendar, same time, twice a week for 4 weeks

The Action Plan

Four weeks, one focus each — plus day 30

1

Week 1 — Build the habit (days 1–7)

You and AI, daily. Every writing task gets a "prompt first" attempt: emails, quotes, replies, ideas. No automation yet — just reps.

Do this: Day 1 quick win above, then one real task through AI every workday. Save prompts that work.

2

Week 2 — Bank quick wins (days 8–14)

Widen from you to your busiest workflows: meeting notes, review replies, follow-ups. Involve one team member so it's not just "the owner's thing."

Do this: Add an AI notetaker to your calls. Give one teammate your 3 best saved prompts.

3

Week 3 — Automate ONE process (days 15–21)

Pick your most repeated, rules-based, hours-eating task (guide #03 finds it). Build the smallest version that works — a lead auto-reply, a follow-up sequence, an intake flow.

Do this: Block two 1-hour sessions. Ship the ugly version by day 21. Ugly and live beats perfect and planned.

4

Week 4 — Measure and lock it in (days 22–29)

Count hours saved, note what broke, fix the automation's rough edges, and write a one-page SOP so it survives without you.

Do this: Fill in the Day-30 Scorecard on page 4. Fix the #1 annoyance in your automation.

5

Day 30 — Decide what's next

With a real number in hand, choose: deepen this automation, start the next one, or bring in help to go faster. Momentum is the asset — don't park it.

Do this: Run your scorecard through the Review Prompt on page 4 and book next month's target.

Common Mistakes

This is where most business owners get stuck.

They skip week 1 and jump to automation with no habits underneath. They pick a week-3 project too big to ship in seven days. And they never count the hours saved — so by month two, nobody remembers why they bothered.

Pro Tip

Tell your team on day 1: "For 30 days we're trying AI on the boring stuff. Nobody's job is changing — we're deleting the parts of your day you already hate." Resistance drops to zero when AI targets the work nobody wants.

The Business Brief (Day 1)

My business: [NAME], a [WHAT YOU DO] serving [WHO] in [AREA]. Our tone: [FRIENDLY / PROFESSIONAL / PLAIN]. Never use hype words or jargon.

The Day-30 Scorecard

  • Hours saved per week: ______
  • Tasks now AI-first: ______
  • Automations live: ______
  • Team members using AI weekly: ______
  • Best win in one sentence: ____________________

The Day-30 Review Prompt

You are my AI operations coach. My business: [WHAT YOU DO]. My 30-day results: [HOURS] hours/week saved, automation built: [WHAT IT DOES], biggest struggle: [STRUGGLE]. Based on this: 1) what should my next 30-day target be, 2) what's the single highest-leverage next automation, 3) what should I stop doing manually immediately? Be specific and blunt.
Stuck in week 3?

Shrink the project until it fits in one hour. "AI drafts the reply, I hit send" is a real automation. Version two can come next month.
