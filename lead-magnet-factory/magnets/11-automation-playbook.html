The One Idea

Automate the handoffs, not the craft.

Your skill stays human. What gets automated is the boring bridge between apps: form → CRM, job done → invoice, invoice paid → review request. Every automation is just a trigger ("when this happens") plus an action ("do that").

Example: A pressure-washing company wired one recipe: website form → instant text reply → lead added to CRM → owner notified. Built on a Saturday morning. They went from "we'll call you back tomorrow" to answering in 30 seconds — without touching a phone.

The Action Plan

Five steps to your first live automation

1

Pick ONE recipe

Not two. The five on page 4 are ranked — Recipe 1 (instant lead reply) pays off fastest for almost every service business.

Do this: Circle one recipe and write its "When ___, then ___" sentence.

2

Map it on paper first

List every step a human does today: where the info starts, where it goes, what gets sent. If you can't draw it, you can't build it.

Do this: Run the Automation Spec Prompt on page 4 — it turns your description into a build plan.

3

Pick your hub and connect your apps

Zapier is easiest, Make is cheaper, n8n is the most powerful. Sign up, then connect the two apps in your recipe (your form tool and your email/SMS, for example).

Do this: Create the account and authorize both apps. That's half the build already done.

4

Build the ugly version and test it

One trigger, one or two actions, plain wording. Send a test lead through it yourself before it ever touches a real customer.

Do this: Submit your own form. Confirm the reply arrives and the data lands where it should.

5

Turn it on, then check it weekly

Go live, then look at the run history every Friday for a month: what fired, what failed, what felt robotic. Tune the wording, then start recipe two.

Do this: Add "check automations" to your Friday routine — two minutes, coffee in hand.

Common Mistakes

This is where most business owners get stuck.

They design a ten-step masterpiece and never finish it. They automate a process that was broken to begin with — now it fails faster. And they set it live and never look again, until a customer gets three duplicate emails.

Pro Tip

Add a "notify me" action to every automation for its first month — a ping to your phone each time it runs. You'll catch weirdness early, and there's no better feeling than watching your business answer a lead while you're at your kid's game.

The Automation Spec Prompt

You are an automation consultant for small service businesses. I want to build: when [TRIGGER], then [ACTIONS]. My tools: forms = [TOOL], email = [TOOL], CRM = [TOOL OR "NONE"]. I'll build it in [ZAPIER / MAKE / N8N]. Give me: 1) the exact step-by-step build order, 2) the message text for each automated send (friendly, under 80 words), 3) what to test before going live, 4) the one failure case most likely to bite me.

Pre-Flight Checklist

  • The manual process works and is written down
  • Trigger and actions fit in one sentence
  • Automated messages sound like us, not a robot
  • I tested it on myself before any customer saw it
  • There's a "notify me" so I see every run this month
Keep the human moment

Automate the instant reply — but make the second touch personal. "System answers in seconds, human calls within the hour" is the combination customers rave about.
