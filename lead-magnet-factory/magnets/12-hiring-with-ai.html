The One Idea

Define the outcome. Screen with a rubric. Decide with your eyes open.

Most job ads list duties. Great ones define outcomes — what a win looks like at 90 days. When the target is clear, AI can write the ad, build the scorecard, and rank the pile. You spend your time only on the top five.

Example: An electrical contractor rewrote "electrician wanted, must be reliable" into an outcome-based ad with three 90-day wins. Applications doubled, and screening 43 resumes against the rubric took one evening coffee instead of a lost weekend.

The Action Plan

Five steps to a hire you're confident in

1

Define the 90-day win

Before any ad goes out: what three things would make you say "best hire I ever made" at day 90? Numbers where possible.

Do this: Write the three outcomes. Example: "Runs jobs solo by week 6."

2

Let AI write the job ad

Feed it your outcomes and your company's personality. You'll get an ad that talks about the candidate's future, not just your requirements list.

Do this: Run the Job Ad Prompt on page 4. Edit once for voice, then post it.

3

Screen with the rubric, not your mood

Score every applicant against the same five criteria. Add one small instruction in the ad (like "start your note with the word BLUE") to instantly filter who actually reads.

Do this: Use the rubric on page 4. Only the top 5 scores get a call.

4

Interview with structure

Same questions, same order, every candidate — behavioral questions about what they've done, not what they'd hypothetically do. Score answers 1–5 as you go.

Do this: Generate your question set with the Interview Kit Prompt on page 4. Print it, score on paper.

5

Onboard like you mean it

The hire isn't done at "yes." Week one decides whether they stay two years or two months. Reuse guide #09's onboarding structure — it works for employees too.

Do this: Ask AI for a 30-day onboarding plan from your three outcomes. Book the day-7 and day-30 check-ins now.

Common Mistakes

This is where most business owners get stuck.

They write the ad about their needs instead of the candidate's future. They hire the best interviewer instead of the best worker — structure beats charm. And they let AI make the final call; it screens, you decide.

Pro Tip

Keep every scored rubric, even from candidates you rejected. Next opening, you have a warm bench of silver medalists — and your fastest, cheapest hire is often someone you already ranked #2 six months ago.

Your hiring kit — ad, rubric, and interview prompts

The Job Ad Prompt

You are a recruiting copywriter for small service businesses. My business: [WHAT YOU DO] in [AREA], team of [N]. I'm hiring a [ROLE]. A great hire delivers these in 90 days: [OUTCOME 1], [OUTCOME 2], [OUTCOME 3]. Pay: [RANGE]. Write a job ad that: 1) opens with what's in it for them, 2) describes a real week in the role, 3) lists must-haves (max 5) and nice-to-haves, 4) ends with how to apply, including this instruction: "Start your application with the word [CODEWORD]." Friendly, plain language, no corporate clichés.

The Screening Rubric  score 1–5 each

CriteriaScore
Followed the codeword instruction___
Evidence they've done this work before___
Signs of reliability (tenure, references)___
Communication quality in their application___
Fit with the 90-day outcomes___
Total (call top 5 scores)/25

The Interview Kit Prompt

Build a structured interview kit for a [ROLE] at my [WHAT YOU DO] business. The 90-day outcomes: [PASTE THEM]. Give me: 1) 6 behavioral questions ("tell me about a time…") tied to those outcomes, 2) what a 5/5 answer sounds like for each, 3) 2 red flags to listen for, 4) a simple scoring sheet I can print. Keep the language plain.
Fair play note

Use AI to organize and score against job-related criteria you define — never to guess age, background, or anything personal. Better decisions, cleaner process, zero shortcuts on fairness.
