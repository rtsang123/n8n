Executive Summary

Half your meetings are status updates. Status updates don't need a meeting.

This playbook runs a 10-minute audit on your recurring meetings, gives every survivor a decision-first format, and hands the note-taking and follow-up to AI.

The One Idea

Meetings are for decisions. Updates are for writing.

Before any meeting earns a slot, it must name the decision being made. No decision? It becomes a written update that people read in two minutes. AI drafts the update, takes the notes, and chases the actions — so even the meetings you keep cost less.

Example: A property management team replaced their Monday hour-long status meeting with a written Monday morning update (AI-drafted from each person's bullet points) plus a 20-minute decision huddle. Same alignment, 40 minutes back — for seven people.

The Action Plan

Five steps to half the meetings, twice the decisions

1

Audit every recurring meeting

List them all with their real weekly cost: length × people. A "quick 30-minute sync" with four people is two hours of payroll.

Do this: Fill the worksheet on page 4. Total the hours — brace yourself.

2

Kill, shrink, or convert

No decision made in the last month? Kill it or convert it to a written update. Decision-makers only in the room; everyone else reads the summary after.

Do this: Tag every row K / S / C / Keep. Act on at least two tags today.

3

Make 25 minutes the default

Change your calendar's default event length. Meetings fill the container you give them — give them a smaller container and agendas suddenly appear.

Do this: Calendar settings → default duration → 25 minutes. Two clicks, permanent savings.

4

Put AI in every surviving meeting

A notetaker (Fathom, Otter, Fireflies) captures notes, decisions, and action items — so nobody "didn't catch that" and you never write minutes again.

Do this: Connect the notetaker to your calendar so it auto-joins everything.

5

End every meeting with the 3-line close

Last two minutes, always: What did we decide? Who does what by when? What's blocked? AI formats it from the transcript and sends it to everyone.

Do this: Run the Recap Prompt on page 4 on your next meeting's transcript.

Common Mistakes

This is where most business owners get stuck.

They cancel meetings but keep the confusion — written updates must replace them, not silence. They invite everyone "to keep them in the loop" when a summary does that for free. And they collect AI meeting notes nobody ever reads — notes exist to chase actions, not to be archived.

Pro Tip

Block one meeting-free morning per week — for everyone, including you. Protect it like a client appointment. It routinely becomes the most productive four hours in the company, and your team will defend it harder than any perk.

The 25-Minute Agenda

MEETING: [NAME] — DECISION NEEDED: [THE ONE DECISION] • 0–5 min: context (pre-read sent yesterday) • 5–18 min: options + discussion • 18–23 min: decide + assign owners • 23–25 min: the 3-line close No decision listed = no meeting booked.

Could this be async? 3 quick checks

  • Is it one-way information? → Written update
  • Is feedback simple yes/no/edits? → Comment thread
  • Real back-and-forth judgment call? → Meeting earns its slot

The Recap Prompt

Here is a meeting transcript from my [WHAT YOU DO] business. Produce: 1) DECISIONS — each in one sentence, 2) ACTIONS — task, owner, deadline as a table, 3) BLOCKED — anything waiting on someone, with who, 4) a 3-sentence summary for people who weren't there. Flag anything discussed for over 5 minutes with no decision reached. Transcript: [PASTE TRANSCRIPT]
