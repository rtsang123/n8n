The One Idea

Record once. Repurpose everywhere.

The hard part of content was never ideas — it's the blank page. Kill the blank page by starting from speech. Answer one real customer question out loud, and the transcript becomes the week's content in every format.

Example: A pool service owner recorded himself answering "why is my pool green even though I shock it?" — 4 minutes in the truck. AI turned it into a Facebook post, two short LinkedIn posts, and a customer email. That one memo brought in three service calls the same week.

Your 10-Minute Quick Win
  • Pick one question a customer asked you this week
  • Record a 3–5 minute voice memo answering it like you would face to face
  • Transcribe it (your notetaker, phone dictation, or paste audio into AI)
  • Run the Repurpose Prompt on page 4 — read your three drafted posts

The Action Plan

Five steps to a weekly engine

1

Build your question bank

List the ten questions customers ask you most. This is a year of content topics — real ones, proven by the fact that people literally ask.

Do this: Steal the starters on page 4, add your own, keep the list in your notes app.

2

Record your weekly memo

Same slot every week — Monday drive, Friday coffee. One question from the bank, answered out loud with a story or example if you have one.

Do this: Put a 10-minute "record memo" block in your calendar, recurring weekly.

3

Run the Repurpose Prompt

Transcript in, content out: posts for your platforms, an email for your list, all in your words with your examples kept intact.

Do this: Paste transcript + prompt from page 4. Edit each draft for 60 seconds max.

4

Schedule the week in one sitting

Batch it: load the posts into your platforms or a free scheduler (Buffer, Later, Meta's own tools) and walk away. Posting daily doesn't mean logging in daily.

Do this: Schedule 3 posts across the week, plus the email if you have a list.

5

Reply and reuse

Comments and DMs tell you what landed — and every reply you write is next week's memo topic. The engine feeds itself.

Do this: Check comments in your email windows (guide #14). Add any new question to the bank.

Common Mistakes

This is where most business owners get stuck.

They post generic AI content with the personality of a manual — starting from a transcript keeps YOUR stories and phrases. They chase five platforms and burn out; one done weekly beats five done once. And they quit at week three, right before familiarity starts compounding.

Pro Tip

End posts with a question, not a pitch. "What's the worst [problem] you've seen?" earns comments; comments earn reach; reach earns enquiries. The algorithm rewards conversation — and every comment is market research you didn't pay for.

Copy / Paste Assets

The prompt, the question bank, the weekly rhythm

The Repurpose Prompt

You are my content editor. My business: [WHAT YOU DO] serving [WHO] in [AREA]. Below is a transcript of me answering a customer question. Keep my phrases, stories, and examples — do not make it sound corporate. Create: 1) a Facebook/LinkedIn post (120–180 words) with a hook first line and a question at the end, 2) two short posts (under 50 words each) pulling the two best single points, 3) a friendly email to my list (under 150 words) with subject line options. Grade 6 reading level. Transcript: [PASTE TRANSCRIPT]

Question Bank Starters

  • What does [service] actually cost — and why does it vary?
  • What's the #1 mistake people make before calling us?
  • How do you choose between [option A] and [option B]?
  • What should you check before hiring anyone in our trade?
  • What surprised this week's customer most?
  • What's a job we fixed that someone else got wrong?
  • When is the RIGHT time of year to do [service]?
  • What can you DIY — and what really shouldn't be?
