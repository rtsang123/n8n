The One Idea

Watch the inputs, not just the bank account.

Your bank balance tells you what already happened. Leads, quotes, and win rate tell you what's ABOUT to happen. Track both ends and you stop being surprised by your own business.

Example: A painting contractor's bank balance looked great in June — best month ever. But his dashboard showed new leads had halved. He turned marketing back on in June instead of discovering the problem in an empty August. That one catch paid for years of 15-minute Mondays.

The Action Plan

Five steps to a business with headlights

1

Build the one-page sheet

A simple spreadsheet: seven rows (the numbers), one column per week. No software to buy, nothing to integrate on day one.

Do this: Copy the page 4 worksheet into Google Sheets. Name it "[Business] Dashboard."

2

Fill in the first week

Pull what's easy (bank, invoices), estimate what's not (hours). Week one is about starting the habit, not audit-grade accuracy.

Do this: Enter last week's seven numbers right now, even roughly.

3

Set a target for each number

What does "good" look like weekly? Ten leads? 40% win rate? Under $5k owed to you? Targets turn a list of numbers into a scoreboard you can win.

Do this: Add a target column. Gut-feel targets are fine — you'll tune them monthly.

4

Run the Monday ritual

Same 15 minutes every week: fill the column, compare to target, ask "what's the ONE number to move this week — and what's the one action that moves it?"

Do this: Write that one action at the bottom of the column. That's your week's focus.

5

Let AI read the trends

Once you have 4+ weeks of data, paste the sheet into AI monthly. It spots drifts and correlations you're too close to see — like win rate sliding every time response speed slips.

Do this: Run the Analyst Prompt on page 4 at month's end. Act on its #1 flag.

Common Mistakes

This is where most business owners get stuck.

They track twenty numbers, drown, and quit — seven is the ceiling, not the floor. They skip "boring" weeks, and the gaps kill the trend line. And they collect numbers without ever picking the one weekly action — a dashboard you don't act on is just prettier anxiety.

Pro Tip

Share the dashboard with your team every Monday — even just a screenshot. When everyone sees the same scoreboard, you stop being the only person in the company who cares whether the business is winning. People play differently when they can see the score.

The Analyst Prompt (monthly)

You are a plain-spoken business analyst for small service businesses. My business: [WHAT YOU DO]. Here are my weekly dashboard numbers for the last [N] weeks: [PASTE THE TABLE]. Tell me: 1) the three clearest trends, in plain words, 2) the one number most likely to cause a problem in 60 days, 3) the single highest-leverage action for next month, 4) one question I should be asking that these numbers can't answer. No jargon.

Make it automatic (later)

  • Leads & quotes: auto-counted by your CRM (guide #05)
  • Cash & A/R: pulled from your invoicing tool's report
  • An automation (guide #11) can fill the sheet weekly
  • Goal: Monday ritual becomes pure thinking, zero collecting
Start manual anyway

Type the numbers by hand for the first month even if you could automate them. Touching the numbers is how they start living in your head — automation comes after understanding.
