## Lesson 1 — Know Your Score (Camera · ~3 min · ~400 words)
**PDF: #01 Business AI Scorecard**

Most owners ask me the same first question: "Which AI tool should I buy?"

Wrong question. The right one is: **"Where is my business leaking the most
hours?"** Because eighty percent of AI purchases go unused — not because the
tools are bad, but because they were pointed at the wrong problem.

So before you spend a dollar, we're going to spend ten minutes. That's what
the Business AI Scorecard does — it's attached below this video.

Here's how it works. Ten statements, five areas: Time, Marketing, Sales,
Operations, and AI Basics. You rate each statement zero to five. Things like:
"Every lead gets a reply within one hour, even nights and weekends." Be
honest — a generous score just hides the leak. Nobody sees this but you.

⏸ **Pause the video right now. Download the scorecard, go to page four, and
rate all ten statements. Gut answers are fine — first instinct is usually
right. It takes about four minutes. Go.**

Welcome back. Now add up your total and look at your five areas. Your lowest
one? That's your bottleneck. And here's the thing most people get wrong: they
try to fix all five at once. Don't. One area, one fix, one win — then
compound.

Page three shows you what your score means. Zero to fifteen: manual mode —
huge upside, best position to be in honestly. Sixteen to thirty: getting
there. Above that, we're automating, not organizing.

And page four has a prompt — you paste your scores into ChatGPT or Claude,
and it hands you a step-by-step plan for your weakest area. We'll use prompts
like that all through this course, and lesson two teaches you how to make
them actually good.

⏸ **One more action before you go. Post your total score and your lowest
area in the community — just like this: "34 out of 50, weakest area: Sales."
That's it. You'll see you're not alone, and I read every one of these.**

Your score is your starting line. In four weeks, you'll re-take this and
watch the number move.

Next lesson: your first real AI win — in the next ten minutes.

"I tried ChatGPT. It was generic."

Today you get the fix: seven copy-paste prompts that already work, and the

off a cliff. Every prompt in this pack has all four built in.

[SCREEN: page 4 — the Business Brief]

But first, the thing that makes every prompt smarter: your Business Brief.
Three sentences — what you do, who you serve, how you sound. You write it

⏸ **Pause here. Fill in the Business Brief on page four — the brackets show

⏸ **Your turn. Pause the video. Pick ONE prompt from page four — the email

## Lesson 3 — Stop the Website Leaks (Loom, screen-share · ~3.5 min · ~460 words)
**PDF: #06 The 15-Minute Website Audit**

Your website isn't losing traffic. It's losing **trust** — and trust is
lost in the first five seconds.

Here's the brutal math: a visitor gives your homepage about five seconds.
In that time they need three answers: what do you do, is it for them, and
what should they click. Miss one, and they hit the back button — straight
to your competitor.

The good news? You don't need a redesign. You need about three fixes, and
today you'll find out which three. Open the audit PDF below.

[SCREEN: pull up a real service business homepage — yours or a volunteer
member's]

Before the checklist, the five-second test. Watch — I'll show this page to
you for five seconds, then ask: what do they do, and what would you click?
[Do it on screen.] If you hesitated, so does every customer.

[SCREEN: page 4 — the 10-point checklist]

Now the ten checks — one point per yes. Three groups. **Clarity:** does a
stranger instantly know what you do? **Proof:** is a real review visible
without scrolling — or is your hard-won reputation buried on page three?
**Friction:** can someone contact you in one step, on a phone, with a thumb?

⏸ **Pause the video. Open YOUR homepage on your phone — not your laptop,
your phone, because that's where your customers are. Run the ten checks on
page four and total your score. Five minutes. Be honest. Go.**

Welcome back. Whatever you scored, here's the order of operations, and it's
always the same. Fix the headline first — page four gives you the formula:
result, for who, in what area, one clear action. "Same-day AC repair for
Raleigh homeowners — book online in sixty seconds." No "Quality You Can
Trust." Nobody's ever booked a job off that headline.

Then move your best review up next to that headline. Then cut your contact
form down to three fields. Capture the lead first — get the details on the
call.

And if you want AI to do the heavy lifting: the rewrite prompt on page four.
Paste in your homepage text and your audit score, and it hands you five new
headlines plus your top three changes ranked by impact.

⏸ **Post your score out of ten in the community — and if you're brave, drop
your website link with it. Members roasting each other's homepages, kindly,
is some of the most valuable feedback in this group.**

Next lesson is the biggest money-maker in this entire course: what happens
in the first hour after a lead contacts you. Don't skip it.

## Lesson 4 — Never Lose a Lead Again (Loom, screen-share · ~4 min · ~500 words)
**PDF: #10 The Lead Follow-Up Blueprint**

This is the highest-ROI lesson in the course. Not because it's clever —
because it's math.

You're already paying for leads. Ads, SEO, referrals, the website you just
fixed — every enquiry costs you something. And then most of them die in
your inbox. Not because the customer said no. Because touch number two
never happened.

Two facts. One: reply within the first hour and you are **many times** more
likely to win the job — the fastest responder beats the best business,
almost every time. Two: most buyers need five or more contacts before they
decide — and most businesses stop at one.

So the fix has two parts: speed, and persistence. Both are systems. Both
are in the PDF below.

First, the uncomfortable part.

⏸ **Pause the video and do this right now: go to your own website and
submit an enquiry, like a customer would. Time how long until a human
responds. That number is your baseline — and for most businesses it's
somewhere between "hours" and "tomorrow." Go find out yours.**

Back? Don't feel bad — feel motivated. Because look at page four.

[SCREEN: page 4 — the five touches]

Five messages, written word for word, ready to steal. **Touch one:** the
instant reply — goes out within minutes, tells them you got their message
and when you'll respond. **Touch two,** day two: a follow-up that leads
with something useful. **Touch three,** day five: proof — a similar job, a
real review. **Touch four,** day nine: the easy nudge — "reply with 1, 2,
or 3." **Touch five,** day fourteen: the breakup — "should I close your
file?" And here's the secret: the breakup gets the most replies of all
five. A polite deadline makes stalled buyers decide.

⏸ **Pause again. Copy Touch One into wherever you answer enquiries — an
auto-reply on your website form, or a saved snippet on your phone. Fill in
the brackets with your details. This one message is worth more than the
rest of the course combined. Five minutes. Go.**

Then, this week, load touches two through five. The Personalizer Prompt on
page four rewrites all five in your voice — paste, run, save as templates.
Set reminders in your CRM, or automate the whole ladder — that's exactly
what we build in lesson seven.

⏸ **Community action: post your test response time — the honest number.
"Submitted at 2pm, heard back 11am next day." Then post it again in a week
after Touch One is live. Watching those numbers collapse is the best
content in this group.**

You've plugged the website and the follow-up. Now let's fill the top of
the funnel — next lesson, marketing that runs on talking instead of typing.

## Lesson 5 — Marketing on Autopilot (Loom, screen-share · ~3.5 min · ~450 words)
**PDF: #15 The Content Engine**

"I know I should post. I haven't in three weeks."

Sound familiar? Here's why it happens: you're treating content like writing.
And writing is slow, so it always loses to real work. Today we delete the
writing.

Here's the engine: you already say marketing gold every single day. Every
customer question you answer — why quotes vary, what to check before hiring
anyone, the mistake you saw on Tuesday — that's content. You just never
write it down. So we won't. We'll **record** it.

[SCREEN: page 4 of the PDF]

The system is three steps. Step one: once a week, record a voice memo —
three to five minutes, answering ONE customer question like they're standing
in front of you. In the truck, on the drive, wherever. Step two: transcribe
it — your phone's dictation or your meeting notetaker does this for free.
Step three: paste the transcript into the Repurpose Prompt on page four. Out
comes a full post, two short posts, and an email — in YOUR words, with YOUR
stories, because it started as your voice. This is the part where AI content
usually goes wrong — it sounds like a manual. Starting from your own
transcript is what keeps it sounding like you.

Watch me do the whole thing once. [SCREEN: play 30 seconds of a memo, paste
transcript + prompt, show the drafts.] One memo. Five pieces of content.
Thirty minutes a week, total.

⏸ **Pause the video. Grab your phone and record your memo right now — answer
the question a customer asked you most recently. Three minutes, rambling is
fine, nobody hears it but the AI. If you're stuck, page four has eight
starter questions. Go record.**

Back? Transcribe it, run the prompt, and read what comes out. Edit each
draft for sixty seconds — fix anything you'd never actually say — then
schedule them across the week. One platform. Not five. One done weekly
beats five done once, every time.

And page four has your weekly rhythm: Monday, record and schedule —
thirty-five minutes all-in. That's your entire marketing department.

⏸ **Community action: before you publish your first AI-drafted post
anywhere, post it HERE. Get feedback from the group, then ship it. And when
it gets its first comment or enquiry — come back and tell us.**

That's the revenue section done: website converts, leads get chased, content
flows. Last module: we make the whole thing run without you. Next lesson —
getting your business out of your head.

## Lesson 6 — Get Out of Your Own Head (Loom, screen-share · ~3.5 min · ~440 words)
**PDF: #07 The Business Systems Checklist**

Quick test. Could you take two weeks off — phone off — and come back to a
business that's still standing?

If that question made you laugh, this lesson is for you. Because right now
you don't fully have a business. You have a very demanding job, and
everything important lives in one place: your head. Every quote, every
process, every "how we do things" — one head. That's not a system. That's
a single point of failure.

Here's the old problem: documenting processes means writing, writing is
slow, so it never happens. And here's what changed: **you don't write
anymore. You talk. AI writes.**

[SCREEN: page 4 — the 8-system checklist]

First, find the weakest link. Page four lists the eight systems every
service business runs on — lead capture, sales and quoting, onboarding,
delivery, billing, marketing, hiring, reporting. For each one, three
checkboxes: Is it written down? Does someone besides you own it? Is any
part automated?

⏸ **Pause the video. Score all eight rows — takes about four minutes.
Then circle your lowest one. Tie-breaker: which system breaks first if you
disappear for two weeks? Go.**

Got your circle? Now the magic part, and it takes twenty minutes total.

Record yourself explaining that process — voice memo, like you're training
a smart new hire. Ten minutes, rambling allowed. Then paste the transcript
into the SOP Builder prompt on page four. Watch. [SCREEN: paste a transcript,
run the prompt.] Out comes numbered steps, the tools needed at each one, a
"done right looks like" checklist, and the three most likely mistakes.
That's a real SOP — the thing you've been "meaning to do" for years —
in one coffee break.

Last step, and don't skip it: put it somewhere your team actually looks.
One folder. "SOPs." Google Drive, Notion, wherever they already live. A
system nobody can find doesn't exist.

Then it's a rhythm: one system per week. Eight weeks from now, your
business runs on paper instead of memory. That's what makes a business
sellable, by the way — and what makes vacations real.

⏸ **Community action: post which system scored lowest for you. You'll see
the same three come up again and again — and the thread where members share
their finished SOPs is pure gold. Add yours when it's done.**

Next lesson is the capstone. Everything you've built — the follow-up, the
content, the SOPs — we now make it run while you sleep. Your first
automation. See you there.

## Lesson 7 — Your First Automation (Camera · ~4 min · ~510 words)
**PDF: #11 The Automation Playbook** (+ #03 Opportunity Finder attached as bonus)

This is the lesson everything else was building toward.

For six lessons, AI has been answering your questions and drafting your
words. That's level one. Level two is different: **automation doesn't wait
to be asked.** A lead comes in at 2am — it gets answered at 2am. An invoice
hits day seven unpaid — a polite reminder goes out. Nobody touched anything.

And here's the shift that makes it click: stop asking "what can AI write
for me?" Start asking **"what happens automatically when a lead comes in?"**
Every automation is just two parts: a trigger — when this happens — and an
action — do that.

[Hold up / show page 4]

The playbook below has the five recipes that pay off fastest for service
businesses, ranked. Recipe one: new lead comes in → instant reply goes out,
lead lands in your CRM, you get a ping. Recipe two: quote sent, three days
of silence → follow-up sends itself. Recipe three: job done → invoice plus
a review request. Recipe four: unpaid invoice → polite escalating
reminders — you never chase money by hand again. Recipe five: meeting
ends → summary and action items, filed and sent.

For almost everyone watching, start with recipe one. It's the same
speed-to-lead math from lesson four — except now it runs at 2am, on
weekends, and on the day your best tech calls in sick.

⏸ **Pause the video. Look at the five recipes on page four and circle the
one that plugs YOUR biggest leak. Then write its one-sentence spec: "When
[this happens], then [do that]." That sentence is your build plan. Two
minutes. Go.**

Now, building it. Page four has the Automation Spec Prompt — feed it your
sentence and your tools, and it hands you the exact build order, the
message wording, and what to test before going live. Zapier if you want
easiest, n8n if you want power. Build the ugly version. One trigger, one
or two actions. Send a test lead through it yourself before any customer
sees it. Ugly and live beats perfect and planned — every time.

⏸ **Then post your "When ___, then ___" sentence in the community. I read
every single one, and I'll tell you honestly: build it yourself this week,
or flag it if there's a gotcha you can't see yet.**

And one promise before you start building: if you get stuck mid-build,
don't quit quietly. Post exactly where you're stuck — screenshot and all —
in the community. Between me and the members who've already shipped their
recipes, you'll be unstuck by tomorrow. Nobody builds alone here.

One more short video to wrap up — where you are now, and what's next.
