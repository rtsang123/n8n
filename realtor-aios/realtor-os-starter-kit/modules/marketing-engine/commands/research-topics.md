# /research-topics

Viral-topic research for the owner's niche. Load first:
`context/market.md`, `context/clients.md`, `context/positioning.md`,
`context/content-history.md`, and `content/research/` (skim past reports so
you don't re-recommend last month's topics).

## 1 — Candidate topics

From the context files, list 4–6 candidate topic areas that sit at the
intersection of (a) what their clients repeatedly ask, (b) what's true in
their market right now, and (c) what fits their positioning. Show the list
and let the owner strike or add before researching.

## 2 — Find outliers

The unit of evidence is the **outlier**: a post/video that clearly
outperformed its creator's normal (views/reactions far above their usual),
and ideally the **same topic outperforming for multiple different
creators** — one hit can be luck; three creators winning on one topic is a
signal.

- **With web access:** search each candidate topic for high-performing
  recent content from realtors/creators in comparable markets (other
  suburban Canadian metros count). Look across platforms — short video,
  Facebook, LinkedIn, YouTube. Collect 2–4 outliers per promising topic,
  from different creators.
- **Without web access:** say so plainly and ask the owner to paste links,
  screenshots-worth-of-text, or descriptions of 3–5 posts they've seen blow
  up lately (their feed is the research tool). Also mine
  `context/content-history.md` — their own past hit counts as an outlier.

## 3 — Analyze each outlier

For each one, record: creator + platform, the topic, the exact hook (first
line / first 3 seconds), the format and structure, roughly how it performed
vs the creator's normal, and — most important — **why it worked**: which of
these were doing the lifting?

- Emotion (fear, hope, indignation, pride, curiosity)
- Specificity (real numbers, named neighbourhoods, real scenarios)
- Contrarian take (challenges what the audience assumes)
- Timing (rides a rate change, a headline, a season)
- Identity (audience sees themselves in it: "first-time buyers in …")

## 4 — Report and rank

Write `content/research/YYYY-MM-DD-topic-research.md`:

```markdown
# Topic Research — <date>

## Ranked topics
1. <topic> — why it's a signal (N creators, which levers), and THE ANGLE FOR
   <owner name>: how this topic becomes theirs — localized to their market,
   consistent with their positioning, sayable in their voice.
2. …

## Outlier log
### <topic>
- <creator> · <platform> · hook: "…" · format: … · performance: … ·
  why it worked: …
```

Then show the owner the ranked list with your top pick and ask if they want
to generate content on any of them now (hand off to `/generate-content`).

**Honesty rule:** report only what you actually found. If the evidence is
thin (couldn't verify performance, only one creator), say so in the report
rather than inflating it.
